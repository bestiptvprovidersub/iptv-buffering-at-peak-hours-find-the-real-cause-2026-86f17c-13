IPTV buffers at peak hours because internet providers slow down video traffic. You can fix this by changing your DNS and adjusting player settings.

If the problem stays, a premium service like SubTV IP eliminates buffering.

Cause #1: ISP Throttling → Fix: Use Cloudflare DNS

Many internet service providers (ISPs) limit streaming speed between 6 PM and 11 PM. This is called “throttling.” Your IPTV signal gets delayed, so the video freezes and buffers.

  • Switch your device or router’s DNS to Cloudflare’s: 1.1.1.1. This hides your video traffic from the ISP.
  • Open your IPTV player and set the buffer / cache to 5,000 ms. That gives the player extra time to pre-load the stream.
  • Test the stream during peak hours. If buffering stops, throttling was the issue.

Cause #3: Weak Wi-Fi or Ethernet → Fix: Optimize Network

Wireless interference or a slow ethernet cable creates packet loss. This shows up as buffering exactly at peak hours when neighbors also use the internet.

  • Use a wired ethernet cable (Cat 5e or better) from the router to your streaming box.
  • If you must use Wi-Fi, move the router closer or switch to a 5 GHz channel.
  • Reboot your router once a week to clear memory conflicts.

Most ISPs throttle video traffic during evening peak hours. Changing your DNS to Cloudflare (1.1.1.1) often solves this.
